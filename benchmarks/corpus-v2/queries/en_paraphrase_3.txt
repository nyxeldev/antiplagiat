Market-based economic systems coordinate the decisions of producers and consumers through the price signals generated by supply and demand. When goods become scarce, rising prices attract additional suppliers; when surpluses accumulate, falling prices discourage production. This decentralised mechanism can allocate resources efficiently without any central authority directing the process. However, markets consistently fail when it comes to activities that impose costs on parties outside the transaction, such as pollution, or when goods are non-excludable and non-rival in consumption. Regulatory frameworks, antitrust enforcement, and targeted public provision address these failures. The rise of digital platforms raises new concerns about market power concentration that existing competition law was not designed to handle.
