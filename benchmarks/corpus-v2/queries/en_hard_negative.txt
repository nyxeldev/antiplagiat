Deforestation in tropical regions continues to accelerate despite numerous international conservation agreements signed over the past two decades. Large areas of forest are cleared each year for cattle ranching, palm oil plantations, and expanding agricultural land, which disrupts habitats for countless species. Researchers studying satellite imagery have documented a steady loss of canopy cover in several river basins, raising concerns about long-term rainfall patterns in the surrounding areas. Local communities that depend on forest resources for their livelihoods are often the first to experience the consequences of this change. Conservation groups argue that stronger enforcement of existing land-use regulations, combined with support for sustainable farming alternatives, could slow the rate of forest loss without harming local economies.
