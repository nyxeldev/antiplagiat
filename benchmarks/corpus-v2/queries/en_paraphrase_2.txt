The gradual disappearance of the Aral Sea stands as one of the most dramatic environmental disasters of the twentieth century, illustrating the severe consequences of unsustainable water management. Excessive diversion of river water for agricultural irrigation — particularly for cotton cultivation — caused the sea to shrink to a fraction of its original size over just a few decades. The ecological ripple effects were profound: regional climate became more extreme, soil salinity increased dramatically, and the health of local communities deteriorated. Dust storms sweeping across the exposed seabed spread salt and toxic residues across surrounding farmland. While the full restoration of the sea is beyond reach, targeted interventions have shown that partial recovery is achievable with coordinated international effort.
