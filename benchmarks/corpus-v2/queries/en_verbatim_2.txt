The Silk Road was not a single road but a network of overland and maritime trade routes connecting China, Central Asia, the Middle East, and Europe for over a millennium. The term itself was coined by the German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen in the nineteenth century, though the routes had existed since the Han Dynasty around 200 BCE. Silk was among the most prized commodities traded, but the routes also carried spices, glass, metals, textiles, and agricultural products in both directions.

Medieval trade along the Silk Road was organised through a relay system: merchants rarely travelled the entire length of the route but instead traded goods at key oasis cities.
